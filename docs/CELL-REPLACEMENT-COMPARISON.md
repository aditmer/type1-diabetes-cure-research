# Islet-replacement strategies for type 1 diabetes

**Evidence snapshot: 22 October 2026.** This focused comparison addresses five distinct strategies. It is a scoping synthesis, not a head-to-head comparison or systematic review. Study populations, designs, endpoints, co-interventions, and follow-up differ; the findings do not establish that one strategy is superior. Insulin independence is not called a cure.

## Scientific interpretation and remaining barriers

These reports show that transplanted cells can produce measurable graft function and, in some selected participants, insulin independence. They do not answer the same question in comparable populations: the donor-islet label covers adults with repeated severe hypoglycemia and reports serious reactions, deaths, and loss of graft function after immunosuppression discontinuation over highly variable follow-up; zimislecel is an early uncontrolled study under immunosuppression; the CiPSC report is a one-person autologous case in a participant already receiving immunosuppressants for a prior liver transplant, which prevented assessment of immune responses to the graft; UP421 is one recipient of gene-edited donor islets without immunosuppression; and VX-264 has a sponsor-reported negative efficacy result without a public endpoint-level dataset. Differences in population, cell source, transplant site, co-interventions, outcome definition, and observation time make cross-study ranking invalid. In particular, the Lantidra follow-up range of 0.3–14.5 years makes direct adverse-event rate comparisons with approximately one-year reports inappropriate. Its events are label-reported observations and do not establish that every event was caused by the cell product.

Barriers remain despite the reported successes:

- **Autoimmunity and alloimmunity:** replacing beta cells does not itself show that recurrent autoimmunity is controlled. Allogeneic grafts also face donor-specific immune responses; the UP421 observation is one recipient and 14 months.
- **Immunosuppression burden:** donor-islet and zimislecel outcomes occurred with systemic immunosuppression. The Lantidra label reports loss of islet-cell function after immunosuppression discontinuation in 8/30 recipients. The Cell authors report that the CiPSC participant was already taking immunosuppressants for a prior liver transplant, preventing assessment of immune responses to the autologous graft; this case therefore does not establish immunosuppression-free efficacy.
- **Engraftment, location, and durability:** portal, abdominal-wall, and forearm-muscle sites are not interchangeable. VX-264's endpoint failure underscores that a device-intended immune barrier does not by itself demonstrate sufficient viable, functioning graft tissue; the reason for the observed failure remains unknown.
- **Safety and scale:** infection, malignancy, procedure-related complications, and hematologic toxicity matter alongside metabolic benefit. A single case or small phase 1–2 cohort cannot characterize uncommon or long-latency risks. Donor availability and manufacturing consistency also constrain scale.
- **Outcome comparability:** insulin independence, C-peptide, severe hypoglycemia, HbA1c, and time-in-range are distinct endpoints. Several studies do not publish values or assessment windows for all requested measures; missing reports should not be converted into negative results or assumed success.

The next evidence step is longer follow-up and larger, prospectively defined cohorts with transparent outcome timing, numeric C-peptide and glucose measures, complete adverse-event reporting, and explicit immunosuppression exposure. Comparative trials or carefully designed external comparisons are needed before making claims about relative benefit.
