# Research Charter

**Version 1.0 — 22 October 2026**

## Mission

Build a transparent, reproducible map of scientific and clinical evidence that could contribute to a safe, durable, and accessible cure for autoimmune type 1 diabetes. Identify bottlenecks and test assumptions rather than promote a favored technology.

## Research questions

1. How can clinically useful beta-cell mass be restored or replaced at scale?
2. How can replacement cells survive autoimmune recurrence and alloimmune rejection without unacceptable lifelong immunosuppression?
3. Can endogenous beta-cell function be preserved or regenerated after diagnosis, and in which disease stages?
4. What combinations of cell therapy, immune modulation, devices, and monitoring could provide durable metabolic control?
5. Which outcomes, follow-up periods, safety data, and access conditions would justify the term “functional cure” or “biological cure”?

## Scope

The project covers human clinical evidence, translational/preclinical evidence where it tests a clearly stated mechanism, trial registries, regulatory decisions, manufacturing, safety, durability, and access. It includes prevention/delay programs as context while separating them from reversal of established disease. Relevant modalities include donor and stem-cell-derived islets, encapsulation and immune-evasive cells, immune tolerance/modulation, beta-cell regeneration, and combination strategies.

## Out of scope

The project does not prescribe treatments, recruit participants, collect personal health data, provide trial matching, recommend unapproved products, or imply that speculative mechanisms are near-term cures. No human experimentation, wet-lab work, or clinical intervention is proposed.

## Operational principles

- State whether evidence is human, animal, in vitro, or computational.
- Separate observed outcomes from interpretation and speculation.
- Report sample size, follow-up, comparator/design, eligibility, harms, and funding/conflicts when available.
- Use registry records for trial status and peer-reviewed reports for outcomes; do not use press releases as sole proof of efficacy.
- Record failed, stopped, negative, and inconclusive programs. Explain what they teach without treating failure as proof that a whole modality cannot work.
- Do not conflate insulin independence, remission, prevention/delay, and cure.
- Prefer durable patient-important outcomes and explicit accounting for treatment burden, immunosuppression, device risk, manufacturing, and access.
- Correct errors publicly with a dated change note.

## Definitions used in this project

- **Prevention/delay:** postponing clinical onset or slowing loss of endogenous insulin production; not reversal or cure.
- **Beta-cell preservation:** maintenance of residual endogenous beta-cell function after clinical diagnosis, measured with a stated endpoint such as stimulated C-peptide; this does not by itself restore lost beta-cell mass, reverse stage 3 disease, or demonstrate remission.
- **Reversal:** a claim that established clinical disease has been moved back to an earlier disease state; name the measured clinical criteria and duration, and do not infer reversal from a biomarker change alone.
- **Partial remission:** reduced insulin need after diagnosis with residual beta-cell function; not cure.
- **Insulin independence:** no exogenous insulin at a stated time point; a measured outcome that may be temporary and treatment-dependent.
- **Functional cure:** project shorthand for durable insulin independence and near-physiologic glucose regulation without chronic systemic immunosuppression, supported by multi-year follow-up and acceptable risk. This is a research target, not a validated regulatory category.
- **Biological cure:** durable restoration of endogenous glucose regulation plus control of the autoimmune process, with acceptable safety and broad feasibility. No current evidence in this repository establishes this outcome.

## Governance and conflicts

This is an individual-led public research project, not an institutional review board, medical authority, or consensus panel. Contributors should disclose relevant financial or professional conflicts. Reviewers should flag unsupported certainty, missing harms, source misrepresentation, and promotional framing. No personal medical stories or identifiable health data should be submitted as issue content.
