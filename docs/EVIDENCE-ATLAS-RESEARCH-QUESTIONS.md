# Evidence Atlas Phase 2 research questions

**Status: proposed design; maintainer approval required**

## Scope and primary question

The Atlas should describe what the eligible evidence reports, how directly it answers a question, and what remains unknown. It should not estimate comparative efficacy, infer missing outcomes, rank therapies, or label an intervention a cure. Human clinical evidence is the primary target for patient outcomes. Registries describe planned designs and status, regulatory records describe actions and indications, and preclinical evidence addresses mechanism only; these evidence contexts are not interchangeable.

**Primary question (RQ-00).** Among people with autoimmune type 1 diabetes (T1D), what human clinical evidence reports durable insulin independence or other patient-important disease-modifying outcomes, in which disease stages and populations, over what observed follow-up, and with what safety burden, ongoing treatment dependencies, retreatment, access constraints, and unresolved uncertainty?

The Atlas must keep three clinical trajectories distinct:

1. **Prevention or delay:** people with presymptomatic Stage 1/2 T1D and progression to clinical Stage 3; postponing diagnosis is not prevention of autoimmunity or reversal.
2. **Preservation:** people with established Stage 3 T1D and residual beta-cell function; maintenance of C-peptide is not restoration of lost beta-cell mass, remission, insulin independence, or cure.
3. **Functional restoration:** people with established Stage 3 T1D receiving cell replacement, regeneration, immune intervention, or combinations; graft activity, insulin independence, and durable glucose regulation are separate outcomes.

## Operational questions

The shared reporting contract below applies to each question. Question-specific endpoints and time anchors refine, but do not replace, the source-defined endpoint. For every human study, record the protocol and actual population, age, disease stage and duration, eligibility, intervention components and dose, comparator, design, enrollment, treated and analyzed denominators, withdrawals, missingness, co-interventions, funding/conflicts, source version, endpoint definition, units, baseline, follow-up distribution, censoring, harms, and limitations. Report planned separately from observed outcomes. Use the project's 12-month observed-follow-up expectation for benefit comparisons beyond early feasibility; shorter studies remain eligible but are short-term. A single 12-month measurement does not establish durability; use multi-year follow-up and ongoing co-intervention reporting before describing an outcome as durable.

| **RQ-01 — Insulin independence and metabolic function.** What proportion achieves source-defined insulin independence, and for how long, while maintaining stated glycemic outcomes? | Established Stage 3 T1D; islet/cell replacement, regeneration, immune modification, or combinations; usual care, active intervention, baseline, or no comparator as actually studied. | Insulin independence and its duration; severe hypoglycemia; HbA1c, CGM measures, exogenous insulin dose, and patient-reported treatment burden. C-peptide and graft measures are supporting surrogates, not substitutes. | Quote the source's insulin-free definition, thresholds, confirmation period, rescue/restart rules, number achieving independence, analysis denominator, start/end dates or interval, relapse/restart, insulin dose and glucose measures at the same stated time points. Anchor follow-up to randomization, intervention, infusion, or independence onset as the source defines. | Human case series through randomized trials, regulatory summaries of primary studies, and registries for planned outcomes only. A press release, single timepoint, unanchored claim, or no denominator cannot answer proportion or duration. |
| **RQ-02 — Severe hypoglycemia.** Does an intervention prevent or substantially reduce severe hypoglycemia and related burden? | Stage 3 T1D, especially populations with impaired awareness or recurrent severe events; any therapy; usual care, active comparator, or baseline where justified. | Severe events and participants affected, emergency assistance, hospitalization, fear/burden, and glycemic control. CGM time below range is a surrogate/context measure, not equivalent to severe events. | Preserve source definition (including assistance requirement), observation window, total event count and distinct participants with ≥1 event separately, recurrent-event handling, person-time if reported, numerator/denominator, baseline history, adjudication, and rescue care. Record pre-specified period and follow-up through at least 12 months for beyond-feasibility comparisons. | Human comparative and longitudinal designs; uncontrolled before/after designs are susceptible to regression to the mean, monitoring changes, and reporting differences. CGM improvement alone, undefined “freedom from events,” or an event total without people affected is insufficient. |
| **RQ-03 — Endogenous insulin production.** Is endogenous secretion preserved, restored, or lost, and how does that relate to clinical outcomes? | Stage 2 at-risk people for prevention questions, recent-onset Stage 3 for preservation, or established Stage 3 for restoration; immune or regenerative therapies and beta-cell replacement; placebo/usual care, active comparison, or baseline. | Clinical outcomes remain insulin needs, hypoglycemia, glycemic control, and quality of life. Surrogates include fasting/stimulated C-peptide and source-defined secretion measures. | Record assay, sample type, stimulation protocol, units, lower limit of detection/quantification, baseline, timepoint, change metric, analysis population, threshold and participant denominator if dichotomized, concomitant insulin and time from diagnosis/intervention. Retain each scheduled assessment, including 12 months and later when reported. | Human trials and cohorts; registry endpoints are planned. Assay variation, disease duration, baseline secretion, age, glucose at sampling, missing tests, and treatment effects on measurement limit comparability. Detectable C-peptide alone, an unvalidated surrogate, or values without assay/timepoint cannot establish clinical restoration or cure. |
| **RQ-04 — Serious harms and discontinuation.** What serious treatment-related or temporally associated harms occur, and how do they affect continued treatment? | All exposed human populations, analyzed separately by stage, modality, and exposure; intervention and each co-intervention; comparator where available. | Serious adverse events, deaths, infections, malignancies, procedure/device complications, discontinuation, graft removal, hospitalization, and treatment burden. Laboratory/biomarker signals are contextual safety measures. | Give event definitions, grade/seriousness, attribution as assigned by source, event counts versus affected participants, denominator exposed, exposure and observation windows, onset, follow-up after stopping, withdrawals and reasons, severity, and handling of recurrent events. Distinguish all-cause from attributed events; include long-term and post-discontinuation observation. | Human trial reports, registries, regulatory safety documents, and post-market surveillance. Rare harms require larger/longer datasets; attribution, underreporting, exposure time, and competing risks limit inference. “No events” in a short or small study, abstract-only claims, or unreported harms are insufficient to establish safety. |
| **RQ-05 — Systemic immunosuppression dependence.** Is the clinical benefit conditional on systemic immunosuppression, and what are the consequences of that dependency? | Cell/graft recipients and immune-modulation populations, with regimen, induction/maintenance, and exposure specified; compare regimens or no immunosuppression only when directly studied. | Insulin independence, graft function, rejection/autoimmunity, infections, malignancy, renal and other toxicity, discontinuation, and quality of life. Drug levels and immune assays are surrogates. | Report agents, dose/regimen, start/stop dates, adherence if available, prophylaxis, changes, cumulative exposure, reasons for stopping, outcomes before/after cessation, and denominator at each timepoint. Anchor to implant/infusion and exposure periods; include multi-year follow-up when claimed. | Human clinical and regulatory evidence; non-human immune-evasion findings are preclinical only. Confounding by indication, selection, and combined regimens limit attribution. A claim of “no immunosuppression” without medication history or an animal experiment is insufficient for a human dependency claim. |
| **RQ-06 — Graft/therapy durability and retreatment.** How long do graft or therapeutic effects persist, and what repeat intervention or rescue is needed? | People receiving replacement, regenerative, or immune therapy; comparator only where direct; stratify by graft type, cell source, device, dose, and co-interventions. | Graft survival/function, sustained clinical benefit, loss of function, repeat infusion/implant, explant, rescue insulin, and re-intervention harms. Biomarkers and device signals are supporting surrogates. | State time origin and definition of graft survival/function, number at risk, censoring, death/withdrawal, failure criteria, repeat dose and dates, competing events, follow-up distribution and median/range, and whether benefit persists after treatment cessation. Report 12 months and each longer available window without assigning the longest observation to all participants. | Human cohorts, trials, registry follow-up and post-authorization studies. Attrition, unequal exposure, informative censoring, multiple grafts per participant, and overlapping reports are key limits. A longest-follow-up anecdote, device explant result without patient outcome, or repeated reports of the same cohort cannot establish durability. |
| **RQ-07 — Population and disease-stage applicability.** For whom do outcomes apply, and where are evidence gaps across stages and subgroups? | Separate Stage 1, Stage 2, recent-onset Stage 3, and longstanding Stage 3 populations; prespecified age, disease duration, residual function, comorbidities, awareness/hypoglycemia history, and prior treatment strata; therapy appropriate to the studied population. | Stage-appropriate outcomes: time to Stage 3 for prevention/delay; C-peptide and insulin needs for preservation; patient-important metabolic, safety, and durability outcomes for established disease. | Include inclusion/exclusion criteria, subgroup definitions, baseline risk, subgroup sample and denominators, interaction analyses and uncertainty, geography/site, access and demographic reporting. Anchor disease stage and duration at enrollment and outcome follow-up to intervention. | Human trials/cohorts and authoritative labels; preclinical models contextualize mechanisms only. Small post hoc subgroups, narrow eligibility, and trial-site selection limit generalizability. Extrapolation from mice, a single recipient, one age/stage, or an unpowered subgroup is insufficient. |
| **RQ-08 — Long-term evidence gaps.** What missing observations prevent assessment of long-term net benefit, safety, or durability? | All populations and modalities with evidence, emphasizing long-term survivors and cohorts with attrition; no comparator required to catalogue gaps. | Missing or incomplete patient-important benefit, late harms, graft failure, retreatment, immunosuppression consequences, mortality, and quality-of-life outcomes. Unreportedness is itself a reporting state, not a negative result. | Identify source access and review scope; planned versus collected versus reported outcomes; follow-up horizon and number at risk; missingness/withdrawal reasons; unpublished/registry outcomes; and unresolved cohort overlap. Keep “not yet extracted,” inaccessible, not reported in reviewed material, and conflicting distinct. | Any identifiable human evidence and registry history; systematic reviews may identify coverage gaps but do not replace primary records. Publication and survivorship bias, inaccessible full text, and stale registry status matter. An empty tracker, registry status alone, or absence of a report cannot establish an outcome was not collected. |
| **RQ-09 — Accessibility and implementation.** What practical conditions constrain safe, broad access to an intervention? | People likely to be eligible and the health systems/manufacturing settings required; all therapeutic classes and delivery strategies; alternatives only as explicitly compared. | Eligibility reach, treatment burden, availability, procedural and monitoring burden, donor/cell supply, manufacturing consistency, repeat treatment, affordability/coverage, geography, and patient-reported outcomes. These are implementation outcomes, not efficacy surrogates. | Record who is excluded and why, delivery setting, specialized staff/infrastructure, production capacity and constraints where documented, procedure and follow-up schedule, travel/access, costs only when source-defined, and jurisdiction/date of regulatory status. Separate documented facts from unmeasured assumptions. | Human implementation studies, health-services research, regulatory/manufacturing documents, and registry metadata with context labels. Sponsor claims, unknown price, and changing policy are limitations. A mechanistic promise or efficacy result alone cannot show broad accessibility. |
| **RQ-10 — Prevention or delay of Stage 3 onset.** In presymptomatic T1D, does an intervention delay clinical diagnosis, and at what safety and treatment burden? | Stage 1/2 people with stated autoantibody and metabolic criteria; prevention/delay or immune-modulating therapy; placebo/usual surveillance or another direct comparator. | Time to Stage 3 diagnosis, cumulative incidence, symptoms/DKA at diagnosis, burden/quality of life, adverse events, and treatment burden. Autoantibody, glycemic, and C-peptide changes are surrogates. | State stage criteria, age and risk criteria, time origin, adjudicated diagnostic criteria, event numerator/analysis denominator, time-to-event method, censoring, median follow-up and range, course/dose, repeat treatment, and all harms. Follow participants through the planned horizon and after onset where reported. | Human randomized trials and longitudinal cohorts; official registry records for design/status and regulatory records for indication only. Surveillance intensity, baseline risk, age, and changing criteria confound cross-study comparisons. Biomarker change without time-to-onset or an animal study is insufficient to answer clinical delay. |
| **RQ-11 — Beta-cell preservation after diagnosis.** In Stage 3 T1D, can residual beta-cell function be preserved, and does that improve patient-important outcomes? | Recent-onset and other explicitly defined Stage 3 populations, separately; immune-modulating or regenerative therapy alongside standard insulin care; placebo/usual insulin care or active comparator. | Insulin dose, hypoglycemia, glycemic outcomes, treatment burden and safety; stimulated C-peptide is a key surrogate. | State diagnosis-to-treatment interval, baseline C-peptide/eligibility, insulin regimen, assay and stimulation, primary endpoint/timepoint, missing data and analysis population, co-interventions and harms. Report at the protocol timepoint and 12 months or longer if observed; never impute a 12-month result from another window. | Human controlled trials and prospective cohorts; registry outcomes are planned until posted as results. Disease course, age, baseline secretion, standard care and missing tests affect interpretation. C-peptide preservation without clinical outcomes cannot establish reversal, insulin independence, or cure. |

## Decision boundary

The questions and minimum reporting fields are a proposed framework, not approved inclusion criteria or a validated outcome set. The Atlas should not compare unlike definitions by silently converting units, cutoffs, populations, time origins, or analysis populations. Missing data stay missing; sources and cohorts are not independent merely because records have different identifiers. Scope, endpoint eligibility, any future harmonization, and real-source integration require maintainer decisions and any other applicable review and governance approvals before implementation.
