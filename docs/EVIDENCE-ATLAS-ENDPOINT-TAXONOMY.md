# Evidence Atlas endpoint taxonomy

**Status: proposed definitions for source-faithful capture; maintainer approval required**

## Capture principles

An endpoint is a source-defined measurement in a specified population, analysis set, and observation window. Store the source wording alongside any Atlas label. Do not convert unlike definitions, thresholds, units, instruments, timing anchors, or analysis populations into a common value. Protocol-specified, observed, post hoc, surrogate, and composite outcomes must remain distinguishable. A missing or inaccessible result is not zero, no event, or a negative result.

For each outcome record: endpoint name and exact definition; planned/observed/reporting state; instrument/assay and method; unit and scale; threshold if used; baseline and follow-up values; time origin and window; enrolled, treated, followed, and endpoint-specific analyzed populations; numerator and denominator for binary participant outcomes; event count and affected-participant count separately for recurrent events; missingness, censoring, and withdrawals; co-interventions; source version/location; and limitations.

The project's **12-month observed-follow-up expectation** applies to benefit comparisons beyond early feasibility, not eligibility. Include shorter studies with a short-term label. Do not fabricate a 12-month value by interpolation, extrapolation, or borrowing a measurement from another window. One 12-month result does not establish durability; long-term claims require multi-year observations, transparent follow-up distribution/number at risk, and disclosure of ongoing treatment.

## Endpoint definitions and required fields

| Endpoint | Definition and source-faithful requirement | Units and analysis rules | Timing anchors and interpretation hazards |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Insulin independence** | No exogenous insulin under the source's exact definition. Capture whether “independent” means no basal/bolus insulin, no insulin at all including rescue, or another protocol-specific threshold; required consecutive days; confirmation method; allowed rescue; and criteria for restarting insulin. Never impose a project-wide threshold when the source has none. | Usually binary participant outcome: number meeting the exact definition / number assessed at the stated time or over the stated interval. Identify treated vs randomized denominator and missing participants. Dose reduction is not independence. | Record onset, verification, each assessment, end/relapse, and time origin (randomization, treatment, infusion, or other). A one-day or short interval is not sustained independence; independence on systemic immunosuppression is treatment-dependent. |
| **Duration of insulin independence** | Elapsed source-defined time during which criteria for insulin independence remain satisfied. Retain the source's interval definition, recurrence rules, and whether periods can be interrupted/restarted. | Days/weeks/months/years or source-reported categories; state participants at risk, number achieving independence, censoring and denominator. Do not convert categories to a mean or assume duration for participants with incomplete follow-up. | Anchor to first qualifying day and state last confirmed independent date, assessment frequency, follow-up end, and treatment exposure. Distinguish follow-up duration from insulin-free duration; maximum follow-up does not apply to all participants. |
| **Severe hypoglycemic events** | Event count under the study's definition (e.g., need for third-party assistance only if the source defines it that way); capture adjudication and event severity. | Report total events separately from distinct participants with ≥1 event; include exposed/observed participants, time at risk/person-time if reported, and recurrent-event method. Do not infer the affected-person count from event count. | Specify the exact observation window, baseline history, adjudication period, and relation to intervention. “No severe events” requires a stated window, population, and ascertainment method. |
| **HbA1c** | Glycated hemoglobin, retaining assay and source-defined value or change. Record baseline and follow-up and whether a threshold is a target, component, or observed outcome. | %, mmol/mol, or source-reported unit; preserve precision and conversion only if the source explicitly reports both. Continuous outcomes need analyzed denominator, summary statistic, dispersion, and missing-data method when available. Do not interpret a threshold composite as an individual-level result without support. | State collection date/window and anchor to treatment/randomization. HbA1c reflects an interval and is not interchangeable with CGM. A nominal “month 12” without an actual schedule/window is insufficient precision. |
| **CGM-derived measures** | Record the metric exactly: time in range (including glucose range, commonly but not universally 70–180 mg/dL), time below/above range, mean glucose, coefficient of variation, or other metric. Capture device, valid-wear criteria, aggregation duration, and analysis method if reported. | Percent of readings/time, mg/dL or mmol/L, or source-defined units; report analyzed participants and CGM data sufficiency. Preserve whether summary is participant-level or pooled readings. Do not equate time in range with HbA1c or hypoglycemia events. | State CGM window, baseline/follow-up dates, time zone/measurement protocol when relevant, and anchor. A reported percentage without aggregation interval or target range is incomplete; avoid comparing incompatible CGM windows. |
| **C-peptide / endogenous insulin secretion** | Fasting or stimulated C-peptide and other secretion measures, preserving assay, stimulation (e.g., MMTT), sampling schedule, AUC/peak metric, detection limit, and source-defined threshold. This is a biological surrogate, not by itself restoration or patient benefit. | Assay units as reported, such as pmol/mL or nmol/L, plus transformation/statistic and analysis denominator. Do not convert units or stimulation metrics unless method and source support an exact conversion; distinguish detectable/not-detectable from quantitative value. | State baseline and post-treatment sampling time, time from diagnosis and intervention, fasting/glucose conditions if reported. Do not infer C-peptide from insulin independence or graft presence. |
| **Exogenous insulin requirements** | Prescribed/administered insulin dose and regimen, including basal/bolus components where available; distinguish insulin dose from dose-adjustment decisions and independence. | Units/day and units/kg/day as source-reported; include body-weight timepoint and denominator, mean/median and dispersion. Report zero only when explicitly measured/reported. Dose change is not comparable without a baseline and consistent calculation. | Anchor baseline and assessment dates to treatment and state averaging window, adherence/measurement method, and co-interventions. A decrease may reflect changed intake, clinical management, or other factors. |
| **Graft survival and function** | Source-defined graft persistence, viability, engraftment, endocrine function, or failure; these are distinct endpoints and should not be collapsed. State whether measure is participant-, graft-, device-unit-, or tissue-level. | Survival probability/participant counts, imaging/biomarker units, or source-defined functional criteria. State number implanted, evaluated, and functioning; denominator may be grafts/devices rather than participants. Do not equate graft persistence with adequate function or clinical benefit. | Anchor to implantation/infusion; report assessments, failure/removal, retreatment, death/withdrawal, censoring and number at risk. Histology from a sentinel device is not automatically representative of the participant or all grafts. |
| **Serious adverse events and discontinuation** | Seriousness and event type per source/regulatory definition; retain investigator attribution separately from temporal association. Capture discontinuation, dose interruption, explant, hospitalization, and death as separate events/reasons. | Distinguish number of events from number of participants with one or more events; provide exposed denominator and event/person-time when reported. Give grade, attribution, and recurrent event handling. “No SAE reported” is not equivalent to no harm if source coverage is incomplete. | Specify exposure period, onset, follow-up after discontinuation and surveillance window; include late events. Small samples and short follow-up cannot rule out rare or delayed harms. |
| **Immunosuppression exposure and complications** | Record each systemic agent, regimen, start/stop, induction/maintenance, changes, and reason; capture infection, malignancy, renal toxicity and other complications separately under source definitions. Distinguish required, planned, received, and discontinued therapy. | Drug dose/levels and units as reported; complications use affected participants and event counts separately with denominators/exposure time. Do not attribute an event to immunosuppression unless the source does. | Anchor exposure to cell infusion/implant and event window; report status at each outcome assessment and after cessation. Absence of immunosuppression in an animal model cannot support a human claim. |
| **Quality of life and treatment burden** | Validated patient-reported quality-of-life, fear of hypoglycemia, treatment burden, functioning, or preference measures; record instrument, domain, language/version, scoring, and minimally important difference only when source-defined. | Scale/range and direction, change metric, analysis population, missing questionnaire count and denominator, summary/dispersion. Do not combine different instruments or treat a surrogate/clinician rating as patient report. | State baseline and collection window/anchor, respondent and recall period. Response/selection bias, unblinding, and missing questionnaires limit interpretation. |
| **Long-term durability and safety** | A longitudinal view of patient-important benefit, graft/therapy function, repeat treatment and delayed harms; it is not a single endpoint or a label of “durable” by itself. | Preserve each constituent endpoint with its own denominator, unit, timepoint, and exposure; include follow-up distribution, numbers at risk, withdrawals, censoring, deaths, and events. Do not pool endpoints or count repeated reports as independent cohorts. | State the time origin, schedule, longest/median follow-up, and which participants reached each horizon. Use “durable” only with multi-year observations and transparent co-interventions; 12 months is a reporting expectation, not a durability threshold. |
| **Stage 3 onset delay** | Time from defined Stage 1/2 baseline to adjudicated Stage 3 diagnosis, kept separate from outcomes after diagnosis. | Event count / analysis denominator plus time-to-event estimate and uncertainty as reported; preserve censoring and competing events. Autoantibody or glycemic changes are not the onset endpoint. | State eligibility/stage criteria, diagnostic rule, time origin, follow-up, surveillance intensity, and post-onset follow-up. Delay is not prevention of autoimmunity or reversal. |

## Analysis-set and event accounting rules

1. Keep **enrolled**, **randomized**, **treated/exposed**, **followed**, and **analyzed for this endpoint** counts separate. A denominator is specific to an endpoint and timepoint.
2. For participant-level binary outcomes, report numerator and the endpoint-specific denominator, with the analysis set and missingness. For continuous outcomes, report analyzed denominator and statistic/dispersion. If unavailable, state “not reported” or “not yet extracted” as applicable; do not infer.
3. For recurrent events, give total events and distinct affected participants separately. If the source reports only one, do not manufacture the other. Event totals can exceed participant counts and are not participant numerators.
4. Preserve source-specific endpoints, thresholds, units, and time anchors. Do not harmonize incompatible measures or use the longest follow-up as if every participant reached it.
5. Separate planned, observed, not reported in reviewed material, inaccessible, not yet extracted, conflicting, and not applicable states. Absence of a result in a registry is not evidence that the measured outcome was zero.
6. Preserve harms and treatment dependencies alongside benefits. Do not infer safety from missing events or cure from insulin independence, C-peptide, glycemic measures, or graft function alone.
